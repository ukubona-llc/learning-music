For the 45-year-old at the keyboard right now
"I could start completely from scratch today at 62 and still win."

Rick Beato is not talking about talent. He is talking about the willingness to enter a new event stream — to start appending new events to a ledger that has never contained piano before, and to let the gradient do its work over time.

The neuroscience agrees with him. Adult brains retain synaptic plasticity far longer than the popular narrative allows. What adults lose in raw acquisition speed, they more than compensate with pattern recognition across domains, disciplined practice structures, and the ability to understand music theory before their fingers can execute it — a profound advantage that children do not have.

The piano is not asking whether you started at five. It is asking what you bring to the next hour of practice. That question has the same answer at 45 as it does at 14: show up, do the work, let the trajectory build.

Statet = Σ Eventi — every session is an event appended to the ledger. The only ledger that stays empty is the one you never open.
